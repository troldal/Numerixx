\chapter{Accuracy, cost, limits and guarantees}\label{ch:guarantees}

This chapter states what to expect of \code{nxx::deriv} and on what evidence: the accuracy per method, order and type;
the cost in evaluations; where the methods stop working; what the library guarantees and under which hypotheses; and
how all of it is tested. Each statement is marked as measured, derived or a rule of thumb, and points to its source.

\section{Accuracy}\label{sec:guarantees-accuracy}

\Cref{tab:accuracy-matrix} gives the measured relative error of seven methods at their default steps
(\code{BackwardRidders} is the mirror of \code{ForwardRidders}), for the first to
the fourth derivative, in three types. The function is $\mathrm{e}^x$, whose derivatives all equal the function, so
that the orders compare; each cell is the median over \num{\valAccuracyMatrixPoints} points $x = 0.5, 0.55, \ldots,
1$, which share the scale $s = 1$ and so the step. The median matters: the rounding part of the error at a single point
is a matter of luck by about a factor of ten, as \code{Central<2>} for $f'$ shows with \num{\valExpCentralTwoAtOneRelError}
at $x = 1$ against a median of \num{\valExpCentralTwoRelError} (measured).

The error model of \cref{ch:stencils} explains the table: a rule of accuracy order P at its default step has an error
of about $\epsmach^{P/(P+N)}$, relative (\cref{tab:default-steps}). Against the double column, the central rules lie
below the model by up to a factor of \num{\valModelRatioCentralBelowMax} for $f'$ to $f'''$ and up to
\num{\valModelRatioCentralFourthAbove} times above it for $f''''$; \code{Forward<2>} lies up to
\num{\valModelRatioForwardTwoAbove} times above it, and \code{Forward<4>} \num{\valModelRatioForwardFourThird} and
\num{\valModelRatioForwardFourFourth} times above it for $f'''$ and $f''''$, by its larger amplification $\ampl$,
which the model drops (measured). In round numbers, \code{Central<2>} in double gives \num{\valHeadlineFirstDigits} correct
digits for $f'$ and \num{\valHeadlineFourthDigits} for $f''''$; Ridders' method \num{\valHeadlineRiddersFirstDigits}
and \num{\valHeadlineRiddersFourthDigits}. \code{ForwardRidders}, whose columns gain one order rather than two,
loses more with each order: on the random study of \cref{sec:guarantees-ridders} its median error is
\num{\valForwardRiddersAccuracyFactorFirst}, \num{\valForwardRiddersAccuracyFactorSecond},
\num{\valForwardRiddersAccuracyFactorThird} and \num{\valForwardRiddersAccuracyFactorFourth} times Ridders' (measured) for $f'$
to $f''''$. Higher orders pay most in the wide type, where the step can grow far before
truncation matters; the float columns show why every float derivative above the first warns, whatever the method.

Two caveats apply to every figure. First, the model gives an absolute error, $\epsmach \abs{f} / h^N$ in its rounding
part, and the relative figures presuppose $\abs{f} \sim s^N \abs{f^{(N)}}$: true for $\mathrm{e}^x$ on $[0.5, 1]$, and
nearly so for the nitrogen $\cp$, where $\abs{\enth} / (s \abs{\cp}) = \num{\valCpRelativeFactor}$
(\cref{ch:rounding}). Near a zero of
$f^{(N)}$ the relative error grows without bound while the absolute error stays (\cref{sec:using-check}). Second, the
default steps assume that $f$ varies on the scale $\max(\abs{x}, 1)$ and is accurate to $\epsmach$; where it varies
faster or is noisier, the figures do not hold until the step is fitted (\cref{fig:step-scale,fig:noise}).

\section{Ridders' error estimate}\label{sec:guarantees-ridders}

Ridders' method returns an estimate of its error, not a bound. Its quality was measured on a random study
(\cref{fig:ridders-coverage}): \num{\valRiddersFunctions} smooth functions with closed-form derivatives ($\mathrm{e}^x$,
$\sin x$, $\log x$, $\sqrt{x}$, $1/x$, $1/(1 + 25x^2)$, $x^5$, $\mathrm{e}^{-x^2}$, $\tanh x$), each at
\num{\valRiddersPointsPerFunction} random points in $[0.2, 3]$, in double, with the default initial steps and no
noise. For $f'$ no singularity lies within the initial step; for $f''$ to $f''''$ the initial step $s/4$ (and the
span $2 h_0$ of $f'''$ and $f''''$) reaches past zero for $x$ below 0.25 (0.5), which are the restarts of
\cref{tab:ridders-statistics}. The table gives the results per derivative order. The
coverage is the fraction of cases whose estimate is at least the actual error; the ratio is actual error over
estimate, so a ratio below 1 is covered.

The estimate is built so that it errs on the safe side: an extrapolated entry's estimate is its change from its parent
in the tableau, but never less than the rounding floor of its level (for the one-sided forms, the floor carried
through the tableau); the result's \code{abs_error} is also at least its distance to the latest diagonal entry. The
method stops when an entry equals its parent, after two levels that improve nothing, or at the resolution of $x$; it
restarts at half the smallest step it tried after a step at which $f$ is not finite and after an untrusted result,
meaning an estimate of at least a quarter of the value and more than 64 times the rounding floor; and it attempts at
most \num{\valRiddersLevelCap} levels in all (\cref{tab:ridders-constants},
\cref{sb:floor-coverage}). The library's tests accept a result whose actual error is at most six times its estimate,
and whose estimate is within twice that of an independent prototype for the same case.

The one-sided \code{ForwardRidders} was measured on the same cases, with its default initial step $s/32$: its
estimate covered \SI{\valForwardRiddersCoverageFirst}{\percent},
\SI{\valForwardRiddersCoverageSecond}{\percent}, \SI{\valForwardRiddersCoverageThird}{\percent} and
\SI{\valForwardRiddersCoverageFourth}{\percent} of them for $f'$ to $f''''$, with worst ratios of
\num{\valForwardRiddersWorstRatioFirst}, \num{\valForwardRiddersWorstRatioSecond},
\num{\valForwardRiddersWorstRatioThird} and \num{\valForwardRiddersWorstRatioFourth}, and median errors of
\num{\valForwardRiddersMedianRelErrorFirst}, \num{\valForwardRiddersMedianRelErrorSecond},
\num{\valForwardRiddersMedianRelErrorThird} and \num{\valForwardRiddersMedianRelErrorFourth} (measured;
\cref{sec:ridders-onesided}). A pole ahead of $x$, inside the span the forward rule samples, lies outside
that study. On $1/(t - a)$ at 0, with \num{\valPoleAheadCases} poles from
$2^{-\num{\valPoleAheadClosestExponent}}$ to $2^{-\num{\valPoleAheadFarthestExponent}}$ ahead, $f'$ and $f''$
were always covered, $f'''$ missed \num{\valPoleAheadUncoveredThird} times (at worst
\num{\valPoleAheadWorstRatioThird} times the estimate), and $f''''$ \num{\valPoleAheadUncoveredFourth} times,
\num{\valPoleAheadSilentFourth} of them neither covered nor flagged: \num{\valPoleAheadSilentRelErrorMinFourth}
to \num{\valPoleAheadSilentRelErrorMaxFourth} times off, with an error up to \num{\valPoleAheadWorstRatioFourth}
times the estimate, within the tests' factor of six. The same poles behind $x$ gave
\num{\valPoleBehindUncovered} uncovered results (measured). Near a known singularity ahead of $x$, repeat a
one-sided $f''''$ at a second initial step.

The estimate assumes that $f$ is accurate to about $\epsmach$. With a relative noise of $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-8}$ on
$\mathrm{e}^x$, the coverage over \num{\valNoiseRiddersCases} points fell to
\SI{\valNoiseTenRiddersCoverage}{\percent} and \SI{\valNoiseEightRiddersCoverage}{\percent}, with worst ratios of
\num{\valNoiseTenRiddersWorstRatio} and \num{\valNoiseEightRiddersWorstRatio} (measured, \cref{sec:rounding-noise}).
It does not see a jump inside its span either, except as a large estimate (\cref{fig:kink}), nor a kink at $x$ at
all (\cref{sec:ridders-trust}).

\section{Cost}\label{sec:guarantees-cost}

The cost of a derivative is counted in evaluations of $f$, which dominate whenever $f$ is more than a few arithmetic
operations; the library's own work is one weighted sum per stencil and one tableau row per level of Ridders' method.
\Cref{tab:cost} gives the counts. For a stencil they are exact: one evaluation per node. For Ridders' method and
\code{ForwardRidders} they depend on $f$ and $x$; the table gives the median and the range of the random study and
the cap, which no call exceeds. \code{BackwardRidders} costs what \code{ForwardRidders} costs on the mirrored
function. For $f'$ in double, Ridders' \num{\valRiddersEvalsMedianFirst} evaluations (median) against a stencil's

\section{Limits}\label{sec:guarantees-limits}

\paragraph{Orders.} The derivative order is 1 to 4. The caps on P are design decisions (\code{docs/deriv/PHASE2_API.md}).
\code{Central<P>} stops at $P = 10$: by the model's exponent, a \code{Central<12>} would cut the error of $f'$ by a factor of about \num{\valCapGainFactorCentralTwelveFirstDouble} in double and
\num{\valCapGainFactorCentralTwelveFirstMultiprecision}
in a 50-digit type (\num{\valCapGainFactorCentralTwelveFourthDouble} and \num{\valCapGainFactorCentralTwelveFourthMultiprecision}
for $f''''$), on a still wider stencil. The one-sided rules stop at $P = 6$, where the rounding amplification $\ampl$
is already \num{\valAmplForwardSix} for $f'$ and \num{\valAmplForwardSixFourth} for $f''''$
(\cref{tab:weights-forward-first,tab:weights-forward-fourth}) and grows about as fast as a higher order pays.

\paragraph{Types.} The integer weights must be exact in \code{T}, which every type with 24 significant bits or more
satisfies; a narrower type is rejected at the call by a constraint. A derivative above the first in a type with an epsilon above
$10^{-7}$ keeps few correct digits, by every method, and warns at compile time (\cref{sec:api-warning}).

\paragraph{The scale.} The default steps assume that $f$ varies on the scale $\max(\abs{x}, 1)$ in the units of $x$.
That is a rule of thumb, not a property of $f$; \cref{sec:using-pitfalls} shows the symptoms when it fails and the cure.

\paragraph{Domain edges.} Ridders' method needs the nearest point at which $f$ is not finite a minimum distance away,
because the levels it spends restarting come out of its budget of \num{\valRiddersLevelCap}. For $\log x$ at
$x = 2^{-k}$ with the default first-derivative step it returned a value down to $k = \num{\valRiddersEdgeLimitExponent}$,
with an estimate grown to flag the loss, but ten correct digits only down to
$k = \num{\valRiddersEdgeTenDigitsExponent}$; it used its whole budget from $k = \num{\valRiddersEdgeCapExponent}$
(measured, \cref{fig:ridders-edge}). For ten digits, keep the nearest bad point at least
$2^{-\num{\valRiddersEdgeTenDigitsExponent}} \max(\abs{x}, 1)$ away. The design record derives the first limit,
$2^{-34} \max(\abs{x}, 1)$, from the initial step $s/32$ and the cap. The same derivation gives
$2^{-31} \max(\abs{x}, 1)$ for $f''$, whose rule reaches $h$ from the initial step $s/4$, and
$2^{-30} \max(\abs{x}, 1)$ for $f'''$ and $f''''$, whose rules reach $2h$; the record's $2^{-31}$ for these two
is a halving too close. On $\log x$, in half halvings, a value was returned down to
$2^{-\num{\valRiddersEdgeLimitExponentSecond}}$ for $f''$ and $2^{-\num{\valRiddersEdgeLimitExponentThird}}$ and
$2^{-\num{\valRiddersEdgeLimitExponentFourth}}$ for $f'''$ and $f''''$ (measured).

A pole, where $f$ is finite on both sides, behaves otherwise. The restart after an untrusted result now
descends as after a non-finite value, so a pole close to $x$ is resolved: at the library's test poles,
$10^{-3}$ ahead of $x$ and $10^{-4}$ behind it, far inside the limits above, \num{\valPoleCovered} of
\num{\valPoleCases} results for $f'$ to $f''''$ were covered, with errors from \num{\valPoleRelErrorMin} to
\num{\valPoleRelErrorMax} (measured). Closer, a pole does not make the method fail: it returns a wrong value.
Over \num{\valPoleSweepCases} poles $1.25$ to $1.75$ times $2^{-k}$ from $x$, $k = \num{\valPoleSweepFirstK}$
to \num{\valPoleSweepLastK}, ahead and behind, $f'$ to $f''''$, none was \code{non_finite} and every one of the
\num{\valPoleSweepUncovered} uncovered results was flagged, its estimate at least a quarter of its value
(measured). Near a pole, test \code{abs_error}.

Exactly at an edge, the one-sided methods apply: \code{Forward<P>} and \code{Backward<P>}, or with an error
estimate \code{ForwardRidders} and \code{BackwardRidders} (\cref{sec:ridders-onesided}). They have a reach of their own: for $\log x$ at $x = 2^{-k}$,
the estimate of \code{ForwardRidders} covered its error up to $k = \num{\valForwardRiddersReachCoveredExponentFirst}$,
\num{\valForwardRiddersReachCoveredExponentSecond}, \num{\valForwardRiddersReachCoveredExponentThird} and
\num{\valForwardRiddersReachCoveredExponentFourth} for $f'$ to $f''''$, and flagged the result, at least a quarter
of its value, from $k = \num{\valForwardRiddersReachFlaggedExponentFirst}$,
\num{\valForwardRiddersReachFlaggedExponentSecond}, \num{\valForwardRiddersReachFlaggedExponentThird} and
\num{\valForwardRiddersReachFlaggedExponentFourth} to the end of the study, $k = \num{\valForwardRiddersReachLastK}$;
between them, $f'$ at $k = \num{\valForwardRiddersReachSilentExponentFirst}$ was
\num{\valForwardRiddersReachSilentRelErrorFirst} off with an estimate of
\num{\valForwardRiddersReachSilentRelEstimateFirst}, relative. The band between the two is narrow but not
always a single point: on $\log x$, $\sqrt{x}$ and $\sqrt[3]{x}$ at four points per halving, \num{\valForwardRiddersBandSilent}
of \num{\valForwardRiddersBandCases} results for $f'$ were neither covered nor flagged
(\valForwardRiddersBandFunctions), each within \num{\valForwardRiddersBandWorstRatio} times its estimate
(measured).

\paragraph{Aliasing.} A function that oscillates on a scale far below the step can lie at every sample; samples that
happen to lie on a polynomial fool every sampling method, Ridders' included (\cref{sb:aliasing}).

\paragraph{Noise and kinks.} A function less accurate than $\epsmach$ needs a larger step (\cref{sec:rounding-noise});
a kink or a jump within a step of $x$ gives a wrong value from every method that samples across it
(\cref{sec:using-pitfalls}).

\paragraph{Overflow.} A final quotient that overflows, such as a fourth derivative with a tiny step, is
\code{non_finite} at run time and a compile error under GCC in a constant expression (\cref{sb:constexpr-overflow}).

\section{Guarantees}\label{sec:guarantees-guarantees}

Each guarantee below is stated with the hypothesis it rests on and with how it is verified.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{No exceptions.} The library throws nothing and catches nothing; failures are \code{std::expected}
    values. An exception thrown by \code{fn} or by the operations of \code{T} passes through unchanged. Verified by
    inspection: the headers contain no \code{throw} and no \code{try}.
  \item \emph{Where \code{fn} is called.} Only at finite points: the overflow checks come before any point is formed.
    A stencil calls \code{fn} once per node, \code{Forward<P>} never below $x$ and \code{Backward<P>} never
    above; a step that is not positive and finite returns \code{non_finite} before any call. Ridders' method
    calls it at most \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsFirst}, \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsSecond}, \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsThird}
    and \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsFourth} times for $f'$ to $f''''$; \code{ForwardRidders} never below $x$ and
    \code{BackwardRidders} never above, restarts included, at most \num{\valForwardRiddersMaxEvalsFirst},
    \num{\valForwardRiddersMaxEvalsSecond}, \num{\valForwardRiddersMaxEvalsThird} and
    \num{\valForwardRiddersMaxEvalsFourth} times. Verified by tests that count and log the calls.
  \item \emph{No shared state.} The headers hold no global, static or thread-local variable other than compile-time
    constants, and \code{fn} is called on the calling thread, one call at a time. Concurrent calls of
    \code{derivative} are therefore as safe as the concurrent calls of \code{fn} they make. Verified by inspection.
  \item \emph{No allocation.} Only fixed-size arrays and \code{std::optional} on the stack; a \code{T} that allocates
    does so in its own operations. Verified by inspection.
  \item \emph{Failures are values, also at compile time.} Every failure is detected before the arithmetic that would
    produce it, so it is an error value in a constant expression too, with one exception: an overflow of the final
    quotient, which GCC rejects at compile time. Verified by \code{static_assert}s on overflowing, non-finite and
    saturating cases.
  \item \emph{Exact weights.} The integer weights are exact in every type with 24 significant bits or more; the
    largest is 6,222,216, below $2^{24}$. A larger value would make the generator's constant evaluation fail, and a
    narrower type is rejected by a constraint of \code{derivative}. Verified at every compilation.
  \item \emph{Exact on polynomials.} With dyadic $x$ and a power-of-two $h$, a stencil is exact for polynomials of degree
    up to $\dorder + \aorder - 1$, provided every intermediate is exact in the type. Verified by \code{static_assert}s
    that also check the bit budget of each case.
  \item \emph{Compile time equals run time.} The same call gives the same bits at compile time and at run time,
    provided the compiler neither contracts \code{a * b + c} into a fused multiply-add nor uses extended precision.
    The build pins neither. Verified on the five toolchains of the continuous integration (GCC, Clang, MSVC,
    clang-cl, Emscripten), by tests that compare constant and run-time results, and by \code{code/07-using-constexpr.cpp}.
  \item \emph{\code{root_epsilon}} is within an ulp of $\epsmach^{1/N}$. Verified against references computed to 80
    digits for selected N from 2 to 2000 in \code{float} and \code{double}, and for the multiprecision types against
    60-digit references.
  \item \emph{Types.} \code{derivative} is tested on \code{float}, \code{double}, \code{cpp_bin_float_50} with and
    without expression templates, and \code{cpp_dec_float_50}, the \code{float} derivatives above the first with the
    warning silenced at the call; \code{long double} satisfies the concept and is tested in \code{root_epsilon} only.
\end{itemize}
Not guaranteed: the accuracy, which is a model and a measurement (\cref{sec:guarantees-accuracy}); Ridders' error
estimate, which is an estimate (\cref{sec:guarantees-ridders}); and the default step, which is a rule of thumb.

\section{How it is verified}\label{sec:guarantees-verification}

The library's tests are one doctest executable and four build tests, listed in \cref{tab:tests}. Their reference
values for the derivatives come from closed forms, from the published tables of Fornberg\cite{fornberg1988} and
Chapra (Chapter 23)\cite{chapra2015} or from an independent
prototype, never from \nxx{} itself; the ceilings on \code{abs_error} for the multiprecision types are twice the
estimates of the first run, regression baselines that \code{docs/deriv/design.md} records as such, not independent
references. Each tolerance is justified by an error estimate in a comment. The stencils,
for example, are held to $(\errc + 2\ampl)\,\epsmach^{P/(P+N)}$ relative on $\mathrm{e}^x$, truncation plus the
rounding of the samples and of the points, for a selection of rules and orders, two-node rules included; the tests'
comment calls it an estimate, not a bound, and reports measured errors 4 to 16 times below it on its three points.
They run on the five toolchains of the continuous integration with warnings as errors. Each phase was reviewed by
specialised review agents, Phases~4 and~5 also by another vendor's model (OpenAI Codex); the findings and decisions
are recorded in the design record (\code{docs/deriv/PHASE3_API.md} to \code{docs/deriv/PHASE5_API.md}, \code{docs/deriv/design.md}). The book's own examples build and run
as tests, and its data generator checks every table against its moment conditions and the nitrogen function against
an independent exact evaluation each time it runs.
