\qrhead{Include.} \texttt{\#include <numerixx/deriv.hpp>}, namespace \code{nxx::deriv}; header-only, C++23; CMake
target \code{numerixx::numerixx} (\cref{sec:api-synopsis}).

\qrhead{The call} (\cref{sec:api-derivative}).
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \code{derivative(fn, x)}: \code{Central<2>}, $f'$, default step;
  \item \code{derivative(fn, x, h)}: your step, \code{h > 0};
  \item \code{derivative<M>(fn, x)}: the method \code{M};
  \item \code{derivative<M, N>(fn, x)}, with or without \code{h}: the derivative order \code{N}, 1 to 4 or
    \code{order::second} and so on.
\end{itemize}
\code{fn} takes a \code{T}; \code{x} fixes \code{T}, any \code{nxx::real} type.

\qrhead{The result.} A \code{std::expected<Estimate<T>, error>}: \code{d->value} is the estimate,
\code{*d->abs_error} Ridders' error estimate (empty for the stencils), \code{d.error()} the reason for a failure.
\code{d.value()} is \code{std::expected}'s accessor and throws on a failure: test, then read \code{d->value}.

\qrhead{Two checks} (\cref{sec:using-result}). \emph{Did it work?} \code{if (d)}. \emph{Is it right?} A wrong step
scale, a noisy $f$ or a break near $x$ return a wrong value without failing: if in doubt, repeat at $2h$ and $h/2$.
For Ridders, also require \code{r->abs_error && *r->abs_error <= atol + rtol * std::abs(r->value)}: \code{rtol}
$= 10^{-n}$ for $n$ figures, \code{atol} in the derivative's units decides near $f' = 0$; a stencil has no estimate to
test. Inside an iteration no check is needed.

\qrhead{Failures.} \code{non_finite}: \code{x} not finite; \code{fn} infinite or NaN at a sample; an overflow; a step
below the resolution of \code{x}. A step $\le 0$ is a bug (an \code{assert}, gone under \code{NDEBUG}). A float
fourth derivative warns at compile time.

\qrhead{Methods} (relative accuracy in double at the default step).
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \code{Central<P>}, P = 2, 4, \ldots, 10: $P + N - 1$ evaluations; about $\epsmach^{P/(P+N)}$; with P = 2
    about \num{\valHeadlineFirstDigits} digits for $f'$ and \num{\valHeadlineFourthDigits} for $f''''$.
  \item \code{Forward<P>}, \code{Backward<P>}, P = 1 to 6: $P + N$ evaluations, never below (above) $x$; larger
    errors than \code{Central<P>}.
  \item \code{Ridders}: a median of \num{\valRiddersEvalsMedianFirst} evaluations for $f'$, at most
    \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsFirst}, \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsSecond}, \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsThird} and
    \num{\valRiddersMaxEvalsFourth} for $f'$ to $f''''$; about \num{\valHeadlineRiddersFirstDigits} digits for $f'$;
    an error estimate. Near a bad point it returns a value down to
    $2^{-\num{\valRiddersEdgeLimitExponent}} \max(\abs{x}, 1)$ away, ten digits only down to
    $2^{-\num{\valRiddersEdgeTenDigitsExponent}} \max(\abs{x}, 1)$.
\end{itemize}

\qrhead{Your own step.} \code{default_step<M, N>(x)} is the one \code{derivative} uses;
\code{h = nxx::math::root_epsilon<3, double>() * w;} for $\epsmach^{1/3} w$
(\code{Central<2>}, $f'$; in general \code{root_epsilon<P + N, T>()}), and
\code{h = std::cbrt(eta) * std::max(std::abs(x), 1.0);} for a noisy $f$.

\qrhead{No error estimate? Check it} (\cref{sec:using-check}). Repeat at $2h$ and $h/2$ and count the agreeing
digits, or compare with \code{Central<4>} or with Ridders' method. Where $f' \approx 0$, judge the absolute error.
Check the sign against the physics.

\qrhead{The pitfalls that matter most} (\cref{sec:using-pitfalls}).
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \emph{The step's scale is in your units.} The default step assumes that $f$ varies on the scale
    $\max(\abs{x}, 1)$; at a large $\abs{x}$ with a narrow feature, or at $\abs{x} \ll 1$, pass $h$.
  \item \emph{Evaluable is not valid.} A fit sampled outside its range returns numbers: make it return NaN there,
    and use \code{Forward<P>} or \code{Backward<P>} at the edge.
  \item \emph{Evaluable is not smooth.} A kink or a range break within a step of $x$ gives a wrong value from every
    method, without failing; Ridders' estimate flags a jump, nothing flags a kink at $x$: differentiate the branch
    you mean.
  \item \emph{\code{auto} and \code{.value()}.} \code{auto e = *d} is an \code{Estimate}, and \code{d.value()}
    throws on a failure; write \code{d->value}.

\end{itemize}
