\chapter*{About this document}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About this document}

This document is a textbook and a reference for \code{nxx::deriv}, the numerical-derivatives module of \nxx{}.
\nxx{} is a header-only C++23 library of numerical algorithms. The module computes the first to the fourth
derivative of a function that a program can evaluate but cannot differentiate: a property model, the output of a
solver or a simulation, or a correlation in someone else's code. The book is written for undergraduate and graduate
students who know calculus and can read C++, but have not studied numerical analysis or floating point. It is meant
to read as a tutorial, one step at a time. The fundamentals are meant to be easy to follow. The topics that a user
does not need, such as proofs, floating-point detail and the library's implementation decisions, are in numbered
sidebars, and the surrounding text never depends on them.

\section*{How to read it}

Part~I teaches. Its seven chapters build the methods up from one idea, Taylor's theorem, and are meant to be read in
order. \Cref{ch:why} shows where derivatives arise and what the library offers. \Cref{ch:taylor} derives the forward
and the central difference and their errors. \Cref{ch:rounding} explains why a smaller step is not always better,
and how the step is chosen. \Cref{ch:stencils} adds more points and one-sided rules, and \cref{ch:orders} treats the
second to fourth derivatives. \Cref{ch:ridders} presents Ridders' extrapolation and its one-sided forms, the methods
that estimate their own error. \Cref{ch:using} shows the library in use, with worked engineering examples and the
pitfalls. One example runs through Part~I: the heat capacity of nitrogen, computed from its enthalpy. Its derivative
is known, so that every error quoted could be measured.

Part~II is the reference. \Cref{ch:api} lists every public name, and \cref{ch:tables} every weight and constant.
\Cref{ch:guarantees} says what accuracy and cost to expect, where the methods stop working, and what the library
guarantees. The appendices hold the floating-point facts and the derivations that the sidebars cite. A notation table
and a glossary follow them.

A reader in a hurry has two shortcuts. \Cref{sec:why-summary}, ``If you read nothing else'', chooses a method for the
five situations that cover most uses, and gives the two checks that every call needs. The
\hyperref[ch:quick-reference]{quick-reference page} (page \pageref{ch:quick-reference}) is the interface on one
sheet: the calls, the result, the failures, the method choice, the checks and the pitfalls that matter most.

\section*{The evidence}

Every number in this book that comes from a computation was produced by the library itself. The only exceptions are
a few figures of its design record and its tests, which are attributed where they appear. A generator program in the
book's sources runs \code{nxx::deriv}. It writes the values, the data behind every plot and the bodies of the tables,
and the text quotes them by name. At every run it also checks the weight tables and the nitrogen example against
independent evaluations.

Every code listing is quoted from a compiled program, which runs as a test of the library and checks what the text
claims about it. There are two exceptions. The declarations of \cref{ch:api} are transcribed from the library's
header, and every build checks them against it. The CMake snippet of \cref{sec:using-call} is quoted from a file that
the book's build does not configure. The data behind every plot and table, and the program that computes them, are
in the repository, under \code{docs/deriv-book/data} and \code{docs/deriv-book/tools}.

\section*{The version}

This document describes \code{nxx::deriv} as of commit \code{90d83378863c} of the \nxx{} repository, branch
\code{dev}; the full hash is in the snippet of Section~7.1. The library's public headers, its tests and its design
record are the ground truth. Where this book and the code disagree, the book has the bug.
