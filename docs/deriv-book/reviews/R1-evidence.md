### R1-evidence-03 (major): the six-digit calculator's ratio is read as the opposite of what it shows

- **Location:** `chapters/03-rounding.tex:64-65` (PDF p. 13, section 3.2).
- **Problem and evidence.** "From h = 0.1 to 0.01 the error falls by a factor of \valHandCalcErrorRatio [21], close to

- **Suggested change.** "... falls by a factor of only 21, not the 100 that truncation alone would give: at h = 0.01
  truncation is about 4.5e-5 of the 2.2e-4, and the rest is already rounding." Generate the truncation term (for
  example `\valHandCalcTruncationHundredth`, e times 0.01^2 / 6).

### R1-evidence-04 (major): "13 and 8 exact stops, all on x^5" is false for f'

- **Location:** `chapters/06-ridders.tex:135-136` (caption of `fig:ridders-coverage`: "an exact stop; 13 and 8
  cases"), `:292-293` (Sidebar 6.2: "13 and 8 exact stops, all on x^5").
- **Problem and evidence.** In `data/ridders-coverage-N1.csv` the 13 rows with `actual` = 0 are: log 4, x^5 3,
  e^(-x^2) 2, Runge 1, 1/x 1, e^x 1, sin 1. Only 3 are x^5. For a transcendental function an actual error of 0 means
  that the returned double equals the double-rounded closed form; nothing in the data shows these to be exact stops
  (an entry equal to its parent). The generator counts `actual == 0` (`generate_data.cpp:1027`), not stops. For N = 2
  all 8 are x^5, as stated.
- **Suggested change.** Sidebar 6.2: "an actual error of exactly 0 in \valRiddersExactCasesFirst cases for f' (3 on
  x^5, the rest values that happen to equal the rounded closed form) and \valRiddersExactCasesSecond for f'', all on
  x^5"; caption: "An actual error of exactly 0 (13 and 8 cases) is clipped ...". If exact stops are to be claimed,
  detect them from the call log and generate their count per function.

### R1-evidence-05 (major): "under half a digit even for 50-digit types" is unsupported and contradicts the book's model

- **Location:** `chapters/10-guarantees.tex:126-127` (section 10.4, "Orders").
- **Problem and evidence.** "Central<P> stops at P = 10: beyond it the gain is under half a digit even for 50-digit
  types." No generated value, test or derivation supports it, and the book's own model contradicts it. At the
  default step the relative error is about S eps^(P/(P+1)) (section 4.3, Appendix B.4). For `cpp_bin_float_50`
  (eps = \valEpsMultiprecision, log10 eps = -50.28), Central<10> gives eps^(10/11) = 10^-45.7 and a Central<12>
  would give eps^(12/13) = 10^-46.4, while S rises only from 2.28 to 2.45 (computed from the closed-form central
  weights): a gain of about 0.7 digit, against 0.2 in double. The design record's own rule (PHASE2_API.md:115,
  "Central<10> loses about D/11 of a type's D digits") gives D/11 - D/13 = 0.7 digit for D = 50. The sentence is the
  header's (`deriv.hpp:105`) and `design.md:60`; neither shows a measurement.
- **Suggested change.** State the reason with its basis and tag it as a rule of thumb: "beyond it a rule of order 12
  would gain, by the model, about 0.2 digit in double and under one digit in a 50-digit type, while f would have to be
  smooth over an ever wider stencil". Report the header's sentence to the author as a library documentation note,
  like U-4.

### R1-evidence-06 (minor): the noise-estimation procedure is attributed to the wrong More and Wild paper

  (sections 3 to 5, with the central difference in section 4), for which the citation at l. 294 is right.
- **Suggested change.** Add `more2011` (metadata above, verified) and cite it at l. 324; keep `more2012` at l. 294,
  7.9 and 10.7.

### R1-evidence-07 (minor): the complex step: one claim misattributed, one history inverted

- **Location:** `chapters/01-why.tex:73-75` (Sidebar 1.1).
- **Problem and evidence.** (a) "It is a form of automatic differentiation in disguise.[squire1998]" Squire and

  remark; "The complex step is a special case of Lyness and Moler's earlier contour-integral methods, which also give

## Verdict

The evidence pipeline is sound: `data/` regenerates byte for byte from the library, every figure reads the data set it
names, the generated tables check themselves, the examples pass, and of the more than forty macros traced, every one is
computed as its sentence says except one quoted for the wrong case (finding 10). The weaknesses lie in the reading of
the data, not in the data: four statements contradict the generated values beside them (the tail of the f''
histogram, the calculator's ratio, the "exact stops on x^5", the half-digit cap), several comparisons are loosened or
mislabelled (T1, the factor of ten, the whiskers, Ridders' gain), and the AI statement does not yet describe the work
as it was done. Every reference is real and, with two small corrections (`sterbenz1974`, `ieee754-2019`), correctly
entered; the DOI of `colebrook1939` is confirmed; two citations should move to works that contain the claim
(`more2011`, `martins2003`). With the five major and sixteen minor findings resolved, the numbers, figures and
citations will withstand scrutiny; a targeted re-check of the changed sentences in round 2 is enough.
